St. Louis Like You've Never Tasted It

Breakfast, lunch, dinner, and dessert — in one afternoon, on foot, and almost all of it invented right here. Start with a slinger at a neighborhood diner, then eat your way through toasted ravioli, St. Louis-style pizza with its love-it-or-hate-it Provel, gooey butter cake, the sandwich Guy Fieri came here to try, and a flaming dessert finale you won't see coming.

Eat Like a Lou-natic is a 3-hour, 1.75-mile small-group food tour through St. Louis' Central West End, led by a St. Louis local — a compulsive historian, deep-rooted neighbor, and the kind of storyteller who makes you want to keep walking long after your feet say otherwise. Every tasting is a sit-down stop, in air conditioning, at neighborhood mainstays that have been feeding the CWE for decades. Guests typically leave too full for dinner.

This isn't a canned tour of famous landmarks you could find on any travel blog. This is a walk through a living neighborhood, told honestly — the wealth, the beauty, the self-inflicted wounds, and the remarkable people who stitched it back together.

What You'll See & Do

Your 1.75-mile route winds through the heart of the Central West End, beginning at Kingside Diner on North Euclid. Along the way, expect:

  • A full spread of St. Louis classics, breakfast through dessert — every tasting a sit-down stop, in air conditioning
  • Grand hotels and Gilded Age mansions that once hosted European royalty
  • The site of the 1904 World's Fair — "the city we still won't shut up about"
  • The story of St. Louis pizza — and why it's unlike anything else on Earth
  • The Delmar Divide: what it is, how it happened, and why it still matters
  • Architecture that traces 150 years of American ambition and consequence
  • The St. Louis mansion where the cocktail party was born — and a toast to mark the spot

The Food

You don't truly know a neighborhood until you've eaten in it. The menu runs like a full day of eating in St. Louis: a slinger to start, toasted ravioli and St. Louis-style pizza with Provel, gooey butter cake for the sugar rush, the Porchetta Louie that Guy Fieri came here to try, and a flaming dessert finale. These are the regional specialties and "IYKYK" items locals have come back to for years — you haven't been to St. Louis until you've had them.

Neck-cooling towels are available when summer heat is a factor.

The History

In the late 19th century, the Central West End was the epicenter of Gilded Age wealth in America's fourth-largest city. It hosted a World's Fair that drew 20 million visitors. It produced a "Spirit of St. Louis" that crossed the Atlantic. It was home to brewing heirs and the family that gave America two Presidents Bush, to robber barons and poets alike.

It also made choices — about race, about development, about community — whose consequences are still being reckoned with today. We don't dress up the uncomfortable parts. Your guide uses that history to tell the full story of a city that had enormous gifts and didn't always use them wisely, but is finding its way back.

Note: This tour includes candid discussion of St. Louis's racial and social history, including events involving race, social justice, and urban development.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where exactly does the tour start?

Meet at Kingside Diner, 236 N Euclid Avenue in the Central West End. Your TourCWE guide will be waiting for you. Street parking is available nearby, and it's a short walk from CWE MetroLink stops.

Is this tour appropriate for kids?

The tour is best suited for adults and older teens. Content includes historical topics involving race and social justice that may require context for younger children. The 1.75-mile walk and 3-hour duration can also be challenging for young ones.

Can you accommodate dietary restrictions or allergies?

The menu is fixed and can change, so we can't guarantee gluten-free, vegan, or allergy-specific options. A pork substitute is available (it contains dairy) — just let us know when you book. If you have severe allergies, please contact us before booking at info@tourcwe.com.

What is the cancellation policy?

You can cancel up to 24 hours before your tour for a full refund. Cancellations within 24 hours are non-refundable. In the case of severe weather, we will cancel and refund automatically.

What should I wear and bring?

Comfortable walking shoes are essential. Dress for the weather — we walk in all conditions except severe weather. Bring your appetite! We recommend not eating a large meal beforehand. Bring a water bottle.

Is the tour available for private groups?

Absolutely. Private group tours are available for corporate events, bachelorette parties, birthday celebrations, and more. Contact us at info@tourcwe.com to discuss custom dates, sizes, and itinerary options.

How much walking is involved? Is it accessible?

The tour covers approximately 1.75 miles on flat, paved sidewalks, and the route is wheelchair accessible. Most guests with average mobility find it comfortable. Please contact us with specific accessibility questions.
